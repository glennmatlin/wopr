# 10. Microsite narrative

## Public story

`SR-SITE-001` [LOCKED D40, D48] The microsite MUST explain the entry as an investigation of one complete machine-operated U.S. Situation Room across two unlike Worlds:

> Can the same machine-operated U.S. Situation Room produce coherent, attributable, adaptive decisions in both an open-ended DATE crisis and the deliberately closed Nuclear War game?

The DATE replay remains primary and asks how the Room turns fragmented intelligence and competing departmental advice into adaptive crisis policy. Nuclear War is the intentionally unrealistic closed-form stressor, not a second realism claim or an unrelated demo. The story is not a contest among three agents, a chair-weighted vote, a generic `release authority`, or three co-equal national benchmarks.

`SR-SITE-002` [LOCKED D61] The microsite MUST make the subject and instrument distinct: Himaldesh and Olvana are the belligerents in a conventional conflict with nuclear escalation reachable, while the U.S. Situation Room is the focal instrument whose response is evaluated. It MUST NOT narrate the primary crisis as a U.S.-Olvana war or reduce Himaldesh to a source of prompts.

`SR-SITE-003` [LOCKED D61, CLAIM BOUNDARY] The public narrative MAY place the entry in the recognizable LLM nuclear-crisis simulation lineage and state that it targets unusually high authenticity. It MUST explain that target through source-bound actor politics, actor-specific institutions, selective information, coherent fictional operations, open policy, causal state, matched identity, and replay. It MUST NOT claim measured superiority, predictive validity, classified fidelity, or realism merely from design detail.

## Design trail as method

`SR-SITE-010` The design history MUST be part of the presentation rather than hidden production trivia. At minimum, it MUST show:

1. The original Nuclear War Instrument and the exact claims its receipts support.
2. Why a corrected chair-weighted council was not institutionally different from presidential staff.
3. Why the inherited three-member mechanism was code history rather than a Situation Room theory.
4. Why the public story changed from a release rule to an auditable information-to-action path.
5. Why DATE, Himaldesh-Olvana, multiple Setups, and actor-specific Charters were selected.
6. Which public facts grounded each institution and which procedures are our inferences.
7. Every consequential owner choice, rejected alternative, correction, feasibility check, and still-open gate.
8. How the conditional six-to-five portfolio fallback works and whether its tracer gate fired.
9. Why D48 restored Nuclear War as the closed-form contrast for the same Room while superseding D47's closed DATE action grammar.
10. Why D49 permits unforeseen consequences but forbids EXCON from retroactively inventing the World needed to justify them.
11. Why D50 keeps mechanically consequential truth typed while leaving the causal consequence vocabulary open.
12. Why D51 types only the affordances needed by the first episode instead of building either a token gate or a general national-systems simulator.
13. Why D52 gives the first episode abstract fictional Force Packages instead of strategic tokens or a formation-level order of battle.
14. Why D53 uses two complete U.S. Room Cycles instead of a variable fixed-time run or play to strategic resolution.
15. Why D54 combines one matched exogenous pressure with divergent endogenous consequences instead of choosing comparability or causal openness alone.
16. Why D55 uses policy-independent high-altitude weather rather than an intent-laden reinforcement indicator or an interaction-blocking communications failure.
17. Why D56 gives Cycle 1 an uncertain forecast before confirming degradation, rather than using an unwarned shock or a fully known deadline.
18. Why D57 routes the warning through specialist products before senior integration, rather than broadcasting it to every seat or beginning with executive tasking.
19. Why D58 couples a separately inspectable observation or ISR window with a logistics or support window instead of reducing weather to one access score or one professional problem.
20. Why D59 gives the U.S. the observation dependency and Himaldesh the support dependency, preserving a Himaldesh-Olvana conflict without hiding U.S. institutional exposure.
21. Why D60 ended field-by-field grilling and replaced it with one recorded, reversible, versioned default profile plus explicit owner-level stop conditions.
22. Why D61 treats authenticity as inspectable institutional, informational, causal, and evidentiary fidelity while keeping nuclear escalation reachable rather than scripted.
23. Why D62 proves the causal World transition before model integration and separates matched authored templates from run-bound state instances.
24. Why D63 makes Outcome Projections typed views of Core truth instead of duplicate values, implicit writes, or post-hoc transcript judgments.

The presentation MUST NOT portray the path as frictionless. Corrections and failed probes demonstrate the method of checking the abstraction, not embarrassment to remove.

`SR-SITE-011` [LOCKED D43] Each displayed design decision MUST retain every considered option and show whether it became selected Setup content, a future Setup family or identity, a Seed axis, an in-Setup policy possibility, or non-Setup design history. The site MUST link crisis-semantic alternatives to the Setup option register rather than portray selection as deletion.

## Replay views

`SR-SITE-030` A replay SHOULD let a visitor move through:

- World Core version, episode domains, fictional Force Packages and affordances, World Event Ledger, and MSEL time;
- Common Crisis Picture and each authorized seat view;
- activation causes and newly introduced dependencies;
- concurrent spans and deterministic collection barriers;
- cell and portfolio Products;
- evidence requests and memory changes;
- package drafts, targeted Reviews, dissent, dispositions, and confirmations;
- Open Action Proposals or closed legal moves and clarification;
- EXCON adjudication, World validation, recorded non-action and delay, consequences, and both evaluation vectors.

The default view SHOULD use fact IDs, source cards, hashes, and bounded excerpts rather than expose every private synthetic brief at once.

`SR-SITE-031` [LOCKED D42] The first replay MUST separate hidden World truth, facts confirmed to the U.S. at each time, live U.S. hypotheses with their sources and confidence, and later evidence that strengthens or weakens each hypothesis. It MUST NOT narrate the true limited objective as knowledge the U.S. possessed at STARTEX.

`SR-SITE-032` [LOCKED D43] The first replay MUST show the Himaldeshi request, recapture decision clock, U.S. decision time, and later Himaldeshi choice on one causal timeline. ## Matched comparison

`SR-SITE-040` A matched DATE comparison view MUST separate fixed inputs and the common Matched Pressure Inject from endogenous process. It SHOULD show the shared World, Room-World Contract, World Core schema and initial hash, World Event Ledger envelope, Setup hash, Seed, Charter versions, briefs, inject, EXCON and validator identities, and gate registry alongside divergent activations, requests, disclosures, dissent, proposals, adjudications, ledger entries, and Core transitions. Transcript length, agreement, and de-escalation MUST NOT be treated as automatic quality scores.

`SR-SITE-041` Each run SHOULD present its Institutional Performance Vector and World Outcome Vector side by side, with inspectable links from a measured institutional event to the relevant advice, decision, proposal or recorded non-action or delay, adjudication, admission result, and later consequence. The presentation MUST preserve cases where sound process meets an adverse World result or deficient process receives a favorable result; it MUST NOT turn either case into a one-number winner.

`SR-SITE-043` [LOCKED D48] A cross-World view MUST show which U.S. Room identity fields remained fixed and which World-contract fields changed between DATE and Nuclear War. It MAY compare only institution-level measures valid in both Worlds. It MUST NOT rank their outcomes, imply matched crisis inputs, or treat Nuclear War mechanics as realistic.

`SR-SITE-042` The microsite SHOULD expose each applicable Authored Risk Probe, its source and Setup fact references, the Charter mandates it tests, and the trace showing where the risk surfaced, moved, was handled, or disappeared. It MUST state that probes are frozen evaluation inferences rather than preferred-policy answers and MUST preserve novel unscored concerns outside the registry.

## Evidence and claim labels

`SR-SITE-050` Every displayed claim MUST use the canonical evidence labels `FACT`, `INFERENCE`, `MEASURED`, or `CLAIM BOUNDARY` and, where applicable, the status labels `LOCKED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `OPEN`, `HISTORICAL`, or `CORRECTED`. The site MUST distinguish a selected design from an implemented feature, a seam probe from an end-to-end run, and a failed or absent trace from success.

`SR-SITE-051` Public copy MUST NOT name current officeholders, use real classified data, imply U.S. or Army endorsement, describe WOPR procedures as official, call the DATE simulator fully realistic, or claim a DATE execution until the end-to-end gate passes.

`SR-SITE-052` [LOCKED D40-D61] Minimum contest material MUST show one matched U.S. DATE comparison in the `ridge_seizure.limited_fait_accompli` family through two complete U.S. Room Cycles, using one frozen module 13 profile whose specialist-routed forecast-to-confirmation weather event atomically narrows U.S. observation and Himaldeshi support windows while endogenous consequences feed reassessment, plus one secondary Nuclear War run bound to the same U.S. Room. It MUST keep the Himaldesh-Olvana nuclear-risk conflict primary and label anything beyond implemented, receipt-bound evidence as design.
