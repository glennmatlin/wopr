# Reuse one U.S. Room across open and closed Worlds

Status: accepted decision D48; supersedes ADR 0032 for DATE and amends ADRs 0010, 0013, and 0016

The Packet will show one complete U.S. Situation Room operating under two different Room-World Contracts. The primary DATE-backed Himaldesh-Olvana World is realism-oriented and open-ended: the Room may issue any intelligible Open Action Proposal, a distinct creative EXCON may propose unforeseen consequences without deciding for a represented Room, and a deterministic World Validator controls what becomes state. The deliberately unrealistic Nuclear War World is the closed-form contrast: the same Room deliberates over finite legal moves whose consequences remain owned by the existing rules engine.

`Same Room` means the same Charter version, seat registry, mandates, group graph, decision routing, Policy Package contract, model or system condition, and institutional evidence model. World-specific facts, capabilities, active-seat triggers, action interface, adjudication, and consequence mechanics may differ. The same Room does not require the same active roster in unrelated situations.

## Considered options

- Keep the D47 five families as an exhaustive DATE command grammar.
- Use open Room proposals but restrict EXCON to an analytical or fully authored consequence space.
- Build separate Room systems for DATE and Nuclear War.
- Force both Worlds through one universal action ontology.

The first would make natural-language policy commentary around a closed move set. The second remains the explicit feasibility fallback but does not satisfy the selected both-creative target. The third would lose the cross-World institutional test. The fourth would distort DATE around card-game mechanics or weaken Nuclear War's verified legal-move contract.

## Consequences

The five D47 families are no longer an admission boundary for DATE. They may remain non-exhaustive standard handlers or analysis tags, but novelty alone cannot reject a proposal. Missing meaning, authority, capability, causal support, or state consistency may prevent an effect while the attempted proposal remains in evidence.

The creative EXCON role and deterministic World Validator must have separate identities, prompts or rules, artifacts, and receipts. D49 bounds EXCON generation to consequences descended from existing World state and requires a separately validated State Patch. Compared DATE runs freeze these configurations and distinguish Room variation from adjudication variation. The existing Nuclear War action-ID and replay receipts support the closed World only; they do not prove the open DATE contract.

DATE design and its first end-to-end tracer are the current work. The Nuclear War adapter and cross-World presentation follow without becoming prerequisites for choosing DATE's open ontology. World Outcome Vectors are not comparable across the two unlike Worlds; only explicitly shared institutional measures may be compared, with their applicability stated.
