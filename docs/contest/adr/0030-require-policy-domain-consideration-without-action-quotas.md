# Require policy-domain consideration without action quotas

Status: proposed normalization of locked decision D45; wording awaits owner ratification

Every D44 U.S. Policy Package must explicitly consider six domains: diplomacy and private channels; intelligence collection and sharing; defense support and posture; economic and financial measures; public communication; and contingency planning and reassessment. For each domain, the package records an immediate component, conditional component, no-action disposition, or not-applicable disposition, together with the contributing responsible seat or seats, deciding forum, and reason.

These are package-coverage headings, not new seats, cells, authorities, action classes, or a requirement to act. An omitted domain is a visible package-completeness failure rather than tacit approval or an invisible judgment.

## Considered options

- Require at least one action in every domain.
- Declare no minimum coverage and let the Room choose which domains to address.

Both remain non-Setup U.S. system comparison candidates. An action quota would make breadth observable by prescribing artificial measures. No declared coverage would preserve maximal discretion but make deliberate omission indistinguishable from oversight. Required consideration with selective action exposes both coverage and restraint without selecting a preferred policy.

## Consequences

The D44 reassessment point remains mandatory even when the contingency domain selects no additional measure; a no-action or not-applicable disposition cannot erase it. Legal routing, safeguards, and dissent remain mandatory package properties rather than extra policy domains or action quotas. Domains do not map to an exhaustive DATE action vocabulary under D48. The exact proposal envelope, policy content, standard handlers, adjudication trace, and validator serialization remain open.
