# Bound generative EXCON to existing World state

Status: accepted decision D49; amended by D50; resolves OPEN-WORLD-008

DATE uses state-bounded generative EXCON. EXCON may derive unforeseen physical, operational, informational, market, public, and non-Room consequences from the current frozen World state, decided Open Action Proposals, exogenous events, and earlier admitted consequences. It may create new downstream complications and ephemeral or aggregate effects without selecting from an authored consequence catalog.

Every consequence proposal must name its causal parents, state it read, entities affected, proposed writes, timing, evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and plausible alternatives. EXCON may adjudicate reactions only for entities that do not have a represented Room, and only within their recorded relationships and capabilities. It may not rewrite history; add prior intent, authority, relationships, forces, access, or capabilities to make an outcome work; decide for a represented Room; or introduce a new strategic actor carrying unearned prior capacity.

## Considered options

- Permit consequences only from pre-authored hidden affordances.
- Permit broad narrative EXCON to introduce any plausible actor, capability, or event.

The first would leave player proposals open while substantially closing the consequence space. The second would maximize narrative range but make matched comparison and state validity depend on unbounded invention. State-bounded generation leaves the consequence list open while requiring every new fact to descend from the recorded World.

## Consequences

EXCON outputs a consequence proposal rather than truth. D50 refines the artifact boundary: every admitted consequence becomes a World Event Ledger entry, while a proposal that changes the typed World Core must also contain a State Patch. The deterministic World Validator checks references, causal lineage, time, authority and capability bounds, forbidden writes, and state consistency before admission. Rejected proposals, patches, and originating Room proposals remain in the replay.

The validator cannot prove that every open-ended narrative inference is realistic. D50 fixes the typed-Core and open-ledger split, while the exact Core domains, patch operations, machine-checkable constraints, and any separate plausibility review remain OPEN. Public claims must describe state-bounded, auditable adjudication rather than verified real-world fidelity.
